Write a proof-comprehension test for the manuscript below.

Readers will have in front of them *some* exposition of these theorems and proofs: possibly this manuscript, possibly a differently organised writeup of the same mathematics with different numbering and ordering (the notation is the same). They answer each question in one to three sentences.

Write 15 short-answer questions:

- 3 `gist` questions: exactly what is proved (hypotheses, quantifiers, conclusion), what the key idea is, how the argument is organised.
- 8 `step` questions: why a specific step holds, or exactly what it uses. Choose steps where the manuscript gives little or no justification, so that a correct answer requires understanding the argument at that point and cannot be copied from one sentence.
- 4 `transfer` questions: apply a lemma or the construction to a small concrete instance, or say whether and where the argument breaks under a stated modification.

Rules:

- A question must be unanswerable by someone who has not read the proof. Keep the setup minimal and never restate definitions, the content of the step, or anything else that would let a clever outsider work out the answer.
- Identify the step or object you are asking about by what it mathematically is or does ("the step that passes from the diagonal identity to the identity with one argument replaced by a pure power", "the lemma giving injectivity of the operator D"), using the manuscript's mathematical notation where needed. Never identify it by a section, lemma or equation number, or by a heading, name or nickname that this manuscript happens to give it: a reader of a differently organised writeup must be able to tell which step you mean.
- Each question has a short definite answer that can be marked right or wrong.

For each question give `id` (1–15), `type`, `question`, `answer` (the reference answer, one to three sentences) and `must_include` (the one to three specific points an answer needs in order to be marked correct).
